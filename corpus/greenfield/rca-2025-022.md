## Problem statement

During a flash sale the orders topic received about 1,200 messages per second against a normal 300. Two orders-worker replicas processed about 700 per second, so consumer lag grew to 52,000 and order confirmations were delayed.

## Impact

### Description of impact

External customers who ordered during the sale. Orders were accepted, but confirmation emails and stock updates were late. No other service was affected.

### Key metrics affected

No downtime. Confirmations were up to 25 minutes late for about 9,000 orders. No orders were lost and no SLA was breached.

### Customer feedback

112 support contacts asking whether their order went through.

## Immediate resolution

Scaled orders-worker from 2 to 4 replicas. Scaled back to 2 the next morning.

## Analysis

### 5-whys analysis

1st why

- Question: Why were order confirmations late?
- Answer: Consumer lag on the orders topic reached 52,000 messages.

2nd why

- Question: Why did lag grow?
- Answer: Messages arrived at 1,200 per second and two workers processed about 700 per second.

3rd why

- Question: Why were only two workers running?
- Answer: Worker count is fixed and sized for normal traffic.

4th why

- Question: Why was it not scaled before the sale?
- Answer: The sale was not on the capacity calendar, so nobody knew traffic would rise.

5th why

- Question: Why did the first response not help?
- Answer: SOP-QUEUE-001 said to restart workers, which does not add capacity.

### Fishbone analysis

#### Process

- Sales events are not added to the capacity calendar.
- The lag procedure restarted workers instead of scaling them.

- 1st why: Marketing and engineering plan events separately.

#### Tools

- Workers do not autoscale on consumer lag.

## Root causes

Based on the analysis above, the root causes of the incident are:

1. Worker capacity was fixed at normal traffic levels and the sale was not planned for.
2. The lag procedure in use restarted workers, which added no capacity.
